# Draft E2/E3 source limited replay protocols

**Status: preregistration draft only. No E2 or E3 case has been frozen, run, or accepted.** The paper does not supply the exact outlet area, full discharge field, air properties, or detailed boundary conditions. A provisional E2 peak/history reconstruction can be prepared with explicit assumptions, but it must not be called an exact replay. Do not use Restás four-slot dimensions or tune assumptions to match a plotted curve. E1 remains earlier in the validation sequence; no E2 solver run is authorized by this draft.

The target cases are the water simulations in C. Calbrix et al. (2023), “Numerical simulation of aerial liquid drops of Canadair CL-415 and Dash-8 airtankers,” local PDF [Numerical simulation of aerial liquid drops of Canadair CL-415 and Dash-8 airtankers.pdf](../Numerical%20simulation%20of%20aerial%20liquid%20drops%20of%20Canadair%20CL-415%20and%20Dash-8%20airtankers.pdf), DOI [10.1071/WF22147](https://doi.org/10.1071/WF22147). The source transcriptions are [E2_CALBRIX_SOURCE.md](E2_CALBRIX_SOURCE.md) and [E3_CALBRIX_CL415_SOURCE.md](E3_CALBRIX_CL415_SOURCE.md). The project sequence and nearfield scope come from [track2_aerial_drop_experiment_plan.md](../track2_aerial_drop_experiment_plan.md) and [docs/VALIDATION.md](../docs/VALIDATION.md).

## What may be compared

E2 and E3 are numerical nearfield comparisons in the aircraft moving frame. They cover the release and expanding plume (regions A/B in the plan). They do not cover the flight to ground, VOF to parcel handoff, ground maps, L95, useful fraction, fire response, or a fourfold gain. Do not extend an E2/E3 result to those claims.

### Dash-8 source alternatives

For E2-PK, 4.8 m/s is the paper’s approximate peak text value (PDF p. 5, Results, “Tank discharge”). It is deliberately held constant from startup as a characterization input. This is an imposed step at t = 0; it is not the paper’s initial acceleration. Observe at 0.5 s for the reported maximum-velocity condition. Do not interpret the case as a replay of the published history.

For E2-HIST use the 51 samples in the [primary Dash-8 Fig. 4 digitization](../data/derived/calbrix_dash8_fig4_velocity.csv). E2-DIG-0.5-primary-history uses that same input trace; E2-DIG-0.5-independent-history uses the separately implemented [second raster read](../data/derived/calbrix_dash8_fig4_velocity_independent.csv), which shares the stated axis calibration with the primary read. These are separate figure reads, not raw source data. The primary trace peaks at 4.693 m/s at 0.4 s; its 0.5 s ordinate is 4.677 m/s, whereas the text says approximately 4.8 m/s at 0.5 s. Keep both records visible. Do not average the curves, raise the 0.5 s digitized value to 4.8, or shift the peak to improve agreement. Propagate each trace's correlated time/ordinate read bounds as described below.

The history’s ordinate and time read bounds are correlated. Propagate the ±0.03 s time read through the local curve slope as well as the listed ordinate bounds; use the wider ±0.15–0.20 m/s bounds at the initial rise and sharp 3.8–4.2 s decline. Do not treat the bounds as independent random errors. The plotted curve has a low nonzero tail at 4.8–5.0 s, while the prose says complete discharge takes approximately 4 s. E2-HIST stops at 4.8 s only to provide the Fig. 5 image time. It must not report a complete release mass, terminal source condition, or payload. If a later run needs a post-4.8 s trajectory, preregister separate tail and cutoff scenarios from a reviewed source interpretation; do not silently ramp to zero.

The paper calls UL both the mean QL/S and the maximum velocity (PDF p. 3 / journal p. 1517 and PDF p. 5 / journal p. 1519). Applying the plotted scalar uniformly over a patch is a declared boundary approximation. It is not the unavailable spatial profile, and area times velocity is not a reported flow rate.

### CL-415 source alternatives

Before E3-HIST, produce two retained digitizations of the Fig. 4 red and green traces with independent extraction methods. Record page raster or vector source, axes calibration, line and legend handling, point spacing, digitization bounds, and the trace-to-zero endpoint. The graph shows two CL-415 curves; the adjacent paragraph assigns “top” to blue and “bottom” to red, while the legend identifies red as top, green as bottom, and blue as Dash-8 (PDF p. 5 / journal p. 1519). Use the legend mapping as the primary transcription, but retain a sensitivity assignment that swaps the two CL-415 trace labels between top and bottom. Do not treat the paragraph’s blue trace as a CL-415 outlet. The graph’s two curves do not establish histories for four individual exits.

If a provisional four-exit geometry and mapping are constructed, store the mapping as a named assumption with source-coordinate evidence and uncertainties. Candidate assignment scenarios are: (1) legend mapping, with the two plotted CL-415 histories assigned to the geometry’s explicitly identified top/bottom groups; (2) the same geometry with those two histories swapped; and (3) four independent per-exit histories supplied by recovered tank-discharge data, if obtained. Scenarios 1 and 2 are sensitivity cases only: the paper does not establish a top/bottom-to-four-port mapping, equal area, equal split, or that each curve should be copied to two exits. Scenario 3 is the only route to source-resolved four-port flow. If a reviewed geometry cannot identify top/bottom groups, do not run scenarios 1 or 2 as E3 comparisons.

For E3-PK, 6 m/s is a constant-from-startup characterization based on the approximate reported peak. Applying it to all four patches is an equal-velocity sensitivity assumption, not a source-reported patch map or per-port flow split. It does not preserve the reported 0.5 s acceleration or the 0.2 s offset. Each port’s flux still depends on its unresolved area and velocity field. The history case should continue through the digitized CL-415 source’s return to zero, then coast to 3 s for Fig. 5. If the plot does not resolve an endpoint at zero, separate reviewed terminal-tail scenarios are required before the 3 s comparison; no artificial shutoff is allowed.

### Dash-8 geometry hypothesis for a provisional sensitivity case

Fig. 2 (PDF p. 4 / journal p. 1518) labels a 4.44 m lower length in the side view and a 0.30 m feature across the bottom in the front view. Pairing them as an orthogonal rectangular opening gives `S_nominal = 4.44 m × 0.30 m = 1.332 m²`. This is a **figure-derived hypothesis**, not an area reported by the paper: neither view labels those dimensions as the active exit. Fig. 3(b) marks the outlet in red but does not map its aperture to the Fig. 2 labels. Do not present 1.332 m² as a recovered specification.

For an explicitly provisional sensitivity bracket, preserve the 4.44 m length and use widths 0.15 m, 0.30 m, and 0.60 m (`S = 0.666, 1.332, 2.664 m²`). The half/double cases are **project-selected area sensitivities**, not physical uncertainty bounds or paper values. Keep them as separate immutable case IDs. An independent reviewer must approve the geometry interpretation and bracket before a quantitative curve run; if the rectangular-opening interpretation is rejected, this geometry cannot support an E2 comparison. Orient the long axis along the published streamwise direction and the 0.30 m feature across it only as the stated reconstruction assumption.

The `0.30 m` width (`S=1.332 m²`) is the proposed nominal area for the E2-DIG-0.5 comparison matrix, subject to independent geometry approval. The `0.15 m` and `0.60 m` widths are separate sensitivity cases and do not enter the nominal pass/fail gate; do not choose among widths based on which curve fits best. Apply the same area to both source-history inputs and both Fig. 6(b) target reads. If the nominal geometry is rejected, report the area sweep as a geometry sensitivity only and do not claim an E2 curve-comparison pass.

### Explicit provisional air and gravity inputs

For the first E2 case packet, assume dry standard sea-level air at `T=288.15 K` and `p=101325 Pa`: `rho_G=1.225 kg/m³` and `mu_G=1.7894e-5 Pa s`. The NASA [U.S. Standard Atmosphere, 1976](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19770009539/downloads/19770009539.pdf), Table 10, p. 20, gives `rho_0=1.2250 kg/m³` and kinematic viscosity `nu_0=1.4607e-5 m²/s`; `mu_G=rho_0 nu_0=1.78936e-5 Pa s`, rounded here. Choosing that state is a **project assumption**, not a condition reported for the Calbrix calculation or measured for Restás. Use `g_z=+9.80665 m/s²` in the figure-based downward-positive z direction, the standard acceleration defined by [BIPM](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.12.html); this is likewise an assumed reconstruction input, not a locally measured value. Disable surface tension, matching the paper's stated model (PDF p. 3 / journal p. 1517). Keep each as an `assumed` input in the manifest, retain these nominal values unadjusted, and do not tune them against the published curves. A reviewer must approve this provisional state or specify a separately identified alternative before a comparison run. These choices do not recover the paper's dimensionless groups because its exit area and exact operating state remain unknown.

Use each E2-DIG-0.5 primary/independent history trace as a piecewise-linear, uniform-over-patch input in its own run. Compute nominal run-window mass from actual solver phase flux; do not use `S U_L(t)` as a paper-reported flow history or payload. The paper's scalar is inconsistently called mean and maximum velocity, and its pointwise spatial profile is unpublished.

If author files are not available, a drawing reconstruction is an explicitly provisional surrogate, not an exact aircraft geometry. Its polygons, scaling, grouping, and bounds must be reviewed and frozen. A missing or null area, patch map, or source endpoint is a launch blocker for a quantitative source comparison. Do not hide it by substituting the Restás pilot’s four slots, an arbitrary unit-area patch, or a fit to the published penetration curve.

The water values and 50 m/s relative air speed are the only fully specified dimensional fluid/source conditions shared by the first comparison set. The paper's equations list air properties and gravity symbolically; its reported dimensionless values do not uniquely recover the missing dimensional inputs. Tables 2–3 report the maximum ejection condition at 0.5 s: Dash-8 `WeG=23643`, `q=7.90`, `KP=3.0`, `β=1.36`, `KL=0.07`, `β′=2.0`; CL-415 `WeG=85020`, `q=10.52`, `KP=1.0`, `β=1.35`, `KL=0.007`, `β′=2.6`. These are checks on the reported case, not tuning targets. The paper defines `Lc=√S` (PDF p. 7 / journal p. 1521), so the missing exit area prevents exact normalized overlays and contributes to unresolved `WeG`; `q` itself does not depend on `S`, but its exact reconstruction remains limited by unstated air density and the ambiguous mean-versus-maximum meaning of the plotted `UL`.

## Execution and analysis plan

1. **Pre-run source review.** Obtain or reconstruct and independently review the outlet geometry, source mapping, time history, property assumptions, signed frame, domain bounds and boundary conditions. Freeze the case’s evidence classes: reported, digitized, inferred, assumed, or fitted. No fitted values are permitted for a primary case. Preserve alternative source interpretations as separate IDs.
2. **Reference extraction.** The published αL = 0.001 cloud-envelope curves in Fig. 6 Z(y), Fig. 7 normalized penetration, Fig. 8 L(z), and Fig. 9 normalized expansion now have independent figure reads with documented calibrations, bounds, and point-to-nearest comparisons. Do not label a computed αL ≥ 0.9 core envelope as a quantitative curve match: the paper does not publish a corresponding core trajectory/width curve. For E2 cases that reach 1.0 s, retain the body-text/caption alternatives for Fig. 8 at 0.5 and 1.0 s (body PDF p. 7, caption PDF p. 9) and compute both target times. E2-DIG-0.5 is a Fig. 6(b) comparison at 0.5 s only; Fig. 8 is outside its comparison gate. For E3, retain the same Fig. 8 ambiguity. Figure 9 shows 0.5, 1.0 and 1.5 s and does not resolve the Fig. 8 conflict. Keep the caption’s “Eqn 5” typo for the lateral fit visible; the body and coefficients identify Eq. (6). The two E3 Fig. 11(a) raster reads record identified liquid-structure counts, not parcels; the figure is PDF p. 10 / journal p. 1524. Their agreement assesses figure-reading reproducibility, but does not make the counts an equivalent benchmark observable: the paper does not publish its Matlab structure-detection method. Keep Fig. 11 descriptive unless that method is recovered or equivalence is independently demonstrated. Record time choices before seeing CFD output.
3. **Source-only boundary check.** For each outlet, check the imposed velocity table, direction, source knots, actual phase flux, integrated run-window mass, and vector momentum flux. For history cases, align integration with every source knot or resolve piecewise-linear boundary integration exactly. Report the source mass only over the simulated interval; do not call it payload unless the source ends with a supported terminal condition.
4. **Numerical profile.** Run a mesh/boundary setup and short profile before committing to multi-second nearfield histories. Use the plan’s resource workflow: run make doctor before assigning compute; reserve two CPUs; use scripts/run_local.py; serialize any GPU job through the launcher. The current local OpenFOAM 2512 interIsoFoam CPU route has only the P0/P1 four-slot evidence (2,081,200 cells, 16 ranks, about 5.6 GiB sampled peak; P1 took about 2,201 s for 0.12 s). It is not an E2/E3 solver qualification and must not be extrapolated directly to 6.9–8.7 million cells or multi-second cases. Calbrix reports 3240 CPU-hours for CL-415 on 180 processors and 28,800 CPU-hours for Dash-8 on 720 processors (PDF p. 5 / journal p. 1519). Treat those as source scale context, not a local runtime estimate. Profile the actual geometry and solver before a long run; do not assume that the visible RTX 5090 provides GPU VOF support.
5. **Mesh/time refinement.** The paper’s centre/wall mesh sizes do not specify our grid. Start with a resource-bounded 1–3 million-cell setup only as a feasibility profile, then use at least two nearfield resolutions for any benchmark comparison. Do not impose a fixed outlet-cell-count cutoff from the paper; set refinement from the reviewed geometry and resolved source scales, measure penetration, width and breakup sensitivity, and accept the case-specific criterion before the comparison run. Set Δt from measured Courant/interface behavior and source-knot resolution; save fields at the prescribed event times and compact alpha-threshold observables at finer intervals. Halve Δt and compare. Candidate mesh/time budgets in [docs/VALIDATION.md](../docs/VALIDATION.md) (5% mesh change for penetration/width and 2% time change) are not automatic acceptance values: obtain a case-specific reviewer decision and freeze them before the run.
6. **Run windows and outputs.** E2-PK runs to 0.5 s and E3-PK to 1.0 s; save their listed event fields. E2-DIG-0.5-primary-history and E2-DIG-0.5-independent-history are distinct runs through 0.5 s with the selected trace knots and target fields. E2-HIST runs to 4.8 s with the listed event outputs and every 0.1 s source knot. E3-HIST runs to 3.0 s only if its digitized source reaches a defensible zero; otherwise its window ends at the last supported source time and the 3.0 s comparison is blocked. At each saved diagnostic time, write compact α ≥ 0.9 and α ≥ 0.001 envelopes, quantitative Z(y) and/or L(z) outputs only for published observables at matching target times, velocity/flux summaries, source histories, mesh/time metadata, and a checkpoint mass ledger. Also report alpha-envelope projection sensitivity at thresholds `5e-4` and `2e-3`; the paper's `alpha_L=0.001` remains the only comparison threshold, and the factor-of-two alternatives are project-selected robustness diagnostics. Retain full 3D fields sparsely. E3 may also compute the Fig. 11 structure counts only after independently reviewing the paper’s Matlab reconstruction and our structure-identification convention; do not treat a different object detector as the same observable.
7. **Comparison gate.** Before the CFD launch, have an independent reviewer approve each source interpretation and the case-specific acceptance budgets. For Fig. 6(b), the proposed primary score is a profile NRMSE: anchor the streamwise sample grid at the outlet origin `y=0` and define computed `Z_model(y)` as the greatest downward extent over the full transverse span of any liquid-envelope point (`alpha_L >= 0.001`) at that `y`, including detached components; do not shift or realign a computed curve after the run. Reduce multiple digitized `Z` values at a duplicate `y` to their median; sample at equal 0.02 m `y` spacing over the common observed support; linearly interpolate only within contiguous trace segments (never across a gap or beyond an endpoint); calculate `sqrt(mean((Z_model-Z_ref)^2))/(max(Z_ref)-min(Z_ref))` with the denominator evaluated on those same reference samples; and report common-support length divided by the particular target trace's full observed `y` span. The 0.02 m spacing and 90% minimum target-span coverage are candidate values for independent approval. Score each E2-DIG-0.5 input-history variant against both target digitizations, identified separately as `Z-primary` and `Z-independent`; a candidate pass requires all four comparisons to meet the approved budget, with no averaging across input histories or target reads. The nominal `S=1.332 m²` geometry is the only area in this candidate gate; the half/double widths are separate, non-gating sensitivities. Candidate landmarks are the streamwise locations where each profile first crosses `Z=0.5, 1.0, and 2.0 m`; require each crossing location to agree within 15% of the corresponding target `y` location, subject to independent approval. Fig. 9(a) remains a plotted secondary diagnostic only: its ambiguous near-origin fragments and unapproved area hypothesis make it unsuitable as a numeric gate in these cases. [docs/VALIDATION.md](../docs/VALIDATION.md) proposes candidate normalized-curve RMSE no greater than 10%, with landmark locations within 15%; these draft targets are not defaults and require independent approval before launch. The Fig. 4 `U_L` read bounds include correlated time and ordinate uncertainty; the two nominal history runs alone do not propagate that uncertainty into computed profiles. A coherent source-perturbation method and its immutable sensitivity-case matrix therefore remain release-blocking; do not create a pointwise best/worst interval by independently varying neighboring samples. Preserve target read uncertainty by reporting the two `Z` digitizations separately; any additional uncertainty interval requires an approved coherent calibration/read-error model. If figure-read uncertainty is wider than an approved comparison budget, report the result as inconclusive; never widen tolerances after viewing results. If there are too few trustworthy curve points for a numeric gate, limit the result to morphology/trend discussion and say so. Keep one unadjusted case. No E2/E3 result establishes field validity or a ground deposition metric.
8. **Run provenance.** Use a new immutable run bundle per case and retain source files/hashes, assumptions, code revision and dirty state, solver/dependency version, mesh, time step, resource measurements, input hashes, all source and boundary fluxes, mass/momentum residuals, raw observables, digitization overlays, and gate decision. Preserve failed and stopped attempts.

No numerical tolerance is selected by this draft. Before any source comparison, reviewers must justify and freeze the permitted source-curve discrepancy against digitization uncertainty, plus conservation, mesh, time, and resource stop conditions. Do not tune area, flow split, turbulence, timing, mesh, or tolerance after examining comparison curves.

## Readiness recommendation

Prepare separate E2-DIG-0.5 primary/second-history packets next, but do not launch E2/E3 CFD. E2 has two separately produced Figure 4 velocity reads that share the documented axis calibration and a dimensional Fig. 6(b) envelope target at 0.5 s, so the two finite-window scalar-history reconstructions can be compared without averaging their inputs or outputs. They remain blocked on independent approval of the nominal-area hypothesis/sensitivity, provisional air/gravity assumptions, body/domain placement, full boundary/turbulence setup, source-uncertainty propagation, case-specific comparison limits, and the preceding E1 validation gate. Surface tension is disabled in the primary input, matching the paper's stated omission. The longer E2-HIST case is still censored: the digitized curve has a low nonzero tail and the paper's approximately 4 s shutoff statement does not resolve it.

E3 is less ready: the four exits and CL-415 history are visible in the paper, but neither the four patch geometry nor the map from two plotted CL-415 curves to four exits is published. Independent Fig. 4 reads now exist; a reviewed patch/group map and supported end-of-source interpretation remain prerequisites before its history replay. The legend-based and swapped-label scenarios may bound one assignment ambiguity after geometry groups are established; they do not reconstruct the missing per-port source field.

The next scientific readiness gate is a written, independently reviewed geometry/source packet for each case, followed by approved comparison tolerances and a short resource profile. Until then, only source extraction and case-preparation work is ready. Any eventual E2/E3 pass would qualify a nearfield numerical comparison under stated provisional inputs; E4/E5 and a ground-map validation remain prerequisites for claims about aerial delivery to the ground.
