# Validation and acceptance protocol

**Status:** protocol definition for Track 2, 25 September 2026. This document defines E0–E6 acceptance and reports no E1–E6 benchmark or empirical pass. The separate runtime gate notes the FluTAS upstream rising-bubble verification; it is not a project benchmark or validation pass. E0's arithmetic tolerances are defined in [E0: analytical foundation](../experiments/E0.md); they are independent of the draft CFD budgets below. Every proposed percentage budget for a CFD or source-comparison case requires a case-specific justification, review, and acceptance before that case runs. These are planning values, not authoritative defaults. The first CFD pilot is a characterization run and is not blocked by the future VOF-to-parcel budgets. If source measurement or digitization uncertainty is wider than an accepted comparison tolerance, report the result as inconclusive; do not widen the tolerance after seeing the result.

## What the result means

The primary result is a prediction for a specified constant-source model. E0 is numerical verification; E1–E3 are comparisons with published numerical or nearfield results; E4–E5 compare deposition processing with field-derived measurements; E6 is the design comparison. A match to a numerical benchmark is not field validation. A match to nearfield morphology does not validate the descent or deposited ground map. Foam, a built outlet, fire interaction, and post-impact behavior remain outside the claim until the required measured inputs and independent tests exist.

The reported useful-strip score is fixed as follows. At each along-track section, calculate the fraction of prescribed width $W$ where $C(x,y)\ge C_*$. $L_{95}$ is the longest continuous along-track interval $I_{95}$ for which at least 95% of that width qualifies at every section. The 95% condition is the plan's initial declared engineering tolerance; publish sensitivity to this percentage and to map resolution. Equality with $C_*$ qualifies. If two intervals have equal length, choose the one with the smallest along-track start coordinate. If the control has $L_{95}=0$, $G$ is undefined and must be reported as N/A.

\[
f_{\rm useful}=\frac{C_*}{M_0}\int_{I_{95}}\int_{-W/2}^{W/2}\mathbf{1}[C(x,y)\ge C_*]\,dy\,dx.
\]

This counts only threshold-satisfying cells inside the longest valid strip, credits each such cell at the required dose $C_*$, and penalizes gaps and excess application. Report $L_{95}$, $f_{\rm useful}$, actual deposited mass inside that strip, mass elsewhere on the ground, and mass crossing each outer boundary. Keep the source papers' collected/ground fraction as a separate quantity. Do not substitute ground fraction for $f_{\rm useful}$.

The comparison factor is exactly

\[
G=\frac{L_{95,\rm Rest\acute{a}s}}{L_{95,\rm control}}.
\]

For a matched counterfactual, hold $M_0,W,C_*,H,U$, wind and other environmental forcing, scoring resolution, and numerical settings fixed; alter only the release. In each run record whether the stated aircraft speed $U$ is airspeed or ground speed. Report operational-condition comparisons separately. No fourfold gain is assumed, targeted, or established by the planned simulations. Any predicted $G$ remains conditional on the stated model and input assumptions; validation of the built system requires measured outlet and material data plus an independent deposition or calibrated plume comparison.

### Map and scoring contract

- Define $C(x,y)$ as deposited water-equivalent mass per ground area (kg/m²), with explicit map origin, along-track/cross-track axes, cell edges, and resolution. Cell concentrations are accumulated from impact mass divided by cell area. Unhit cells have $C=0$. Freeze the binning and boundary conventions before scoring.
- Freeze $W,C_*,M_0$, 95% width qualification, interpolation/binning rules, and map spacing for a comparison pair. Compute qualifying width by physical overlap with the prescribed strip, not by counting cell centers. For clipped boundary cells, assume the cell's concentration is uniform within that cell and document this representation. Use half-open map bins so interior-edge points are assigned once; include the final outer edge in the final bin. Repeat decisive maps at half and double the nominal cell width; report score changes. Do not smooth, shift, or clip a map to improve its score.
- Accumulate the entire ground map, including material beyond the prescribed strip and target rectangle. Track airborne liquid at the reporting time and each boundary crossing separately. Report domain truncation rather than painting uncomputed mist onto the ground.
- Preserve the original mass weights and impact coordinates through VOF-to-parcel handoff and ground accumulation. Do not count one parcel more than once.

## Conservation and aircraft-to-ground checks

For the constant source, the prescribed payload relation is $M_0=\rho A_{\rm total}|v_n|T$, where $v_n$ is outlet velocity normal to the outlet plane. The outlet direction may change independently of speed. Integrate source mass flux from the actual boundary flux, not from a nominal setting alone. Record the prescribed velocity vector and the area-weighted vector momentum flux for each outlet.

At every VOF-to-parcel transfer plane, compare the incoming liquid mass flux and vector momentum flux with the mass and vector momentum assigned to the outgoing parcels over the same time interval. For the resolved-field side, store the phase-weighted fluxes, including $\alpha_L$; for parcels, store each mass and velocity. Log residuals by transfer interval and cumulatively. Cumulative transferred mass is an audit quantity, not an additional mass compartment. At any checkpoint $t$, the exclusive inventory/sink ledger must close against mass released by that time:

\[
M_{\rm src}(t)=M_{\rm ground,target}(t)+M_{\rm ground,elsewhere}(t)+M_{\rm airborne}(t)+M_{\rm outflow}(t)+M_{\rm evaporated}(t)+M_{\rm other\;documented\;sink}(t),
\]

where $M_{\rm src}(t)=\int_0^{\min(t,T)}\dot M_{\rm src}(\tau)\,d\tau$, so $M_{\rm src}(t)=M_0$ only after the release ends. Terms are omitted only when that process is absent. $M_{\rm airborne}$ is the disjoint current inventory $M_{\rm VOF}+M_{\rm parcels}$ still inside the domain; liquid already transferred is not counted twice. Outflow is cumulative liquid crossing the side/top/bottom domain boundaries without ground deposition. Evaporation is a separate sink if a later model includes it; it is never counted as ground water. Momentum must match across the representation transfer. Subsequent changes from gravity, drag, pressure, and boundary forces are external impulse/flux terms, not transfer error.

Use a right-handed release-frame basis: along track, cross track, vertical. Store aircraft position and attitude in ground coordinates and each liquid impact's position relative to the aircraft-frame origin at impact. For an impact at $t_i$, map it to the ground with the aircraft's ground-frame trajectory and attitude at that impact:

\[
\mathbf{x}_{g,i}=\mathbf{x}_{a}(t_i)+\mathbf{R}_{a\to g}(t_i)\,\mathbf{r}_{a,i}.
\]

For a straight, level aircraft with constant attitude this reduces to translating a position expressed relative to the moving aircraft by its ground displacement through $t_i$. Source values called "aircraft speed" may mean airspeed or ground speed; resolve that convention from the source or mark it unknown. Use measured aircraft ground trajectory when available. If only airspeed is supplied, derive ground velocity from airspeed and wind only after recording the wind-direction/sign convention. If acceleration or attitude changes are later represented, include the corresponding moving-frame terms in the solver and test them explicitly; do not apply aircraft translation twice.

### E0 arithmetic gates

E0 is analytical verification and contains no VOF or parcel-transfer simulation. Use the independent synthetic expectations and exact roundoff bounds in [E0: analytical foundation](../experiments/E0.md): exact matching for counts/indices/zero-strip cases; $10^{-12}$ absolute/relative agreement at order-one scales and a scaled $10^{-10}$ absolute allowance for larger values where justified. Keep the source arithmetic, ballistic solution, coordinate map, weighted deposition, strip score, dose cap, partial-cell behavior, mass ledger, and ellipse arithmetic independent of any CFD acceptance budget. E0 completion is required for trusting and scoring later cases. It does not impose future VOF-to-parcel budget values as a prerequisite for launching the first characterization pilot.

Before each applicable controlled CFD or comparison run, justify each value for its mesh, time step, transfer method, observable, and source uncertainty; have the reviewer accept and freeze the case-specific protocol. The first 5090 pilot measures and reports solver behavior and conservation; it is allowed to run before transfer budgets are accepted. A transfer budget becomes a gate before relying on a VOF-to-parcel production result, not before the first pilot.

### Published-case comparison gates

Before a solver comparison, record the exact source case, units, initial/boundary conditions, figure/table, digitization method, and source uncertainty. Digitize source curves independently twice and preserve both digitizations. Before applying any curve RMSE, freeze its exact formula and normalization denominator, x sampling/interpolation domain, point weighting, treatment of x uncertainty and source intervals, and prohibition on extrapolation. Draft targets are normalized curve RMSE no greater than 10% for penetration and width curves, with breakup/penetration landmark locations within 15%; justify and accept these targets for the specific source and observable before the comparison run. They are not published outcomes or authoritative defaults. If the source provides wider uncertainty or too little numerical data, report the comparison and uncertainty without a pass/fail label.

## Runtime and scale gates

Use these compute gates before committing to large VOF runs:

1. **RTX 5090 pilot (32 GB):** use a GPU-capable VOF implementation in an idealized box with roughly 1–3 million cells and a few hundred steps. Measure peak device memory, time per step, time-step limit, interface/mass error, pressure-solver share, and output cost. The pinned upstream FluTAS base builds and passes its rising-bubble check; that base lacks separate pulsed slots and its GPU pressure solver lacks the usual one-sided inlet/outlet pressure pair. Candidate patches now add a proposed slot source boundary, but they remain unqualified until exact review, source-disabled runtime checks, and the independently reviewed source-boundary gate clear. See the [feasibility record](GPU_FLUTAS_FEASIBILITY.md). A CPU OpenFOAM VOF case is not assumed to run unchanged on GPU.
2. **Extrapolate before scaling:** estimate wall time to one physical second, peak memory, and checkpoint size. Try 5–10 million cells only if the pilot and boundary conditions support it. Run two nearfield resolutions, adding a third when penetration, transfer flux/location, breakup location, or $L_{95}$ fails the accepted case-specific sensitivity gate. Use the 20-core Ultra 7 265K for small independent CPU references and post-processing.
3. **Cluster use:** reserve the approximately 1300 CPU cores for choices that need complex aircraft geometry, local refinement, direct-VOF-to-ground attempts, and selected converged control/design comparisons. Profile a short partition, memory per rank, and scaling first. A uniform centimetre-scale 60 m descent is out of scope: a 10 × 10 × 30 m domain at 1 cm spacing is about 3 billion cells.
4. **Trace every run:** checkpoint short runs; routinely write compact ground maps and transfer-plane records; save full 3D fields only at selected render times. Log input geometry and physics, code revision, grid, seeds, wall time, peak memory, conservation error, and the reason to advance or stop.

## Inputs that block a built-system claim

The current authorized what-if runs may use clearly labelled provisional inputs from the plan: four outlets in a 2 × 2 layout with approximate 1 m × 0.10–0.15 m slot dimensions and an ideal prescribed constant velocity. These are assumptions for E6 preparation, not measured specifications and not evidence about the built system. The required measured packet is: actual outlet dimensions and spacing; water/foam density, rheology, and surface behavior; valve opening and flow/pressure-versus-time traces; payload, attitude, speed and altitude; wind; and test video with camera geometry and timestamps. Foam and CO₂ aeration may need effective properties or an additional phase; water-only runs do not validate foam. No flame or ground-after-impact dynamics are claimed in this version. Until the measured packet and an independent deposition or calibrated plume comparison exist, report the result as a prediction under specified assumptions.
